# Animation study: Eddie's DM reels + saved reels (14 Nov 2026)

Owner asked: study every animated reel in the Eddie DM chat on the SaveSort account and in the saved tab. Look at lighting, shadow, movement, effects, cuts and script, and find what makes animated reels go viral **and** turn into sign-ups.

**How it was studied**
- **9 reels from Eddie's DMs** (downloaded to the owner's "animation reels" folder) were analysed frame by frame at the real 30 fps. For each frame we measured brightness, contrast, warmth, saturation and motion, and found cuts automatically. The audio was transcribed with Whisper.
- **11 saved/linked reels** got 24-frame contact sheets plus their caption and counts (likes / comments / reposts / sends), read from the page. Instagram's player is too slow for a 30 fps pass on long reels, and the audio can't be transcribed without downloading the file. Drop any reel into the "animation reels" folder for the full treatment.
- All numbers below are measured or copied from the post at the time of reading.

**Look and light (all 9)**
- **Pixar 3D is the house style.** Soft, big key light from the front or side, a warm skin tone, and a cooler blue/teal background so the character pops. Shallow depth of field, background softly blurred.
- **Light follows the mood.** Bad moments are darker and cooler (luma ~35–45, mostly shadow). The win or reveal brightens and warms up (warmth R−B jumps, luma up 20–40%). It works like a colour "glow-up".
- **Shadows are soft and under the character** (contact shadow), never hard or noisy. That is a big part of why they don't look like AI slop.
- **The subject sits dead centre** in nearly every frame. There is room for one-word captions in the lower third.

**Movement and cuts**
- Two rhythms win:
  - **Fast (0.8–1.4 s per shot):** short drama and ads. Under-1-second shots are 66–68% of all shots.
  - **Talk-led (2–6 s):** 90+ second stories, where a fast voice (140–170 words a minute) does the work.
- **The setup is slow, the payoff is fast.** Each reel opens with 1–5 s of calm setup, then speeds into a montage at the win (0.2–0.5 s cuts).
- Most shots have a slow push-in or a small handheld drift. Crash zooms go on the reaction faces. Nothing is fully still except the end card.

**Captions and sound**
- **Captions:** one word at a time, centred, white with a black or yellow outline, one key word highlighted.
- **Sound:** the voice is always on top, the music bed is low, and a drop or hit lands on the twist.

## 3. What actually makes them work (ranked by impact for SaveSort)

1. **A comment keyword that sends a real link by DM is the biggest lever for comments and sends.** saveo.house got 22.4K comments and 18.4K sends on 26K likes; adilet got 3.5× more comments than likes. This only works if a tool (ManyChat or Instagram's own auto-DM) **really sends something**. For us that is "Comment SORT and Sorty DMs you the link + how to export your saves in 60 seconds." It is a real reward, so it passes our rule 23. A "comment YES" with nothing behind it does not.
2. **One character, one voice, every shot, named episodes.** The two fastest-growing accounts (saveo.house, the fruit-drama pages) are **series**, not one-offs. People follow to see the next one. This is Sorty's whole job: same design, same voice, same sign-off.
3. **An everyday "that's so them" story beats a clever one.** The 25-second "price went up" reel got 3.5K sends with almost no comments. People sent it to a mate or boss. Our version: the mate with 2,000 gym saves, the partner with 40 recipe reels.
4. **Story Loop structure:** stakes in second one → a question you want answered → the twist → reopen with "Part 1"/next episode. "Part 2" comments are free reach.
5. **The product is the payoff, not an interruption.** The Higgsfield, candy and detailing reels all make the product the answer to the story's problem. For us: Sorty's problem (drowning in saves) gets solved on a real SaveSort screen.
6. **The first second decides everything.** The Higgsfield-detailing copy had the same story as a 17K-like reel and got about 16 likes; it opened on a static gate. Open on a face, a line and a sound.
7. **End on something real.** The detailing reel's last 3 s are real phone footage of real work. Ours: 2 s of the real SaveSort report (real numbers), then savesortai.com.
8. **The look that doesn't read as slop:** Pixar-style soft key light, warm character, cooler background, soft contact shadow, shallow focus, the subject centred, a slow push-in on every shot. Darker and cooler for the problem, brighter and warmer for the win.
9. **Rhythm:** a calm 2–4 s setup, then 0.7–1.5 s cuts, then a 0.2–0.5 s montage on the win. For 30–40 s reels, aim for 25–35 shots (re-used frames and punch-ins count).
10. **Captions:** one word at a time, centred, outlined, one highlight colour per reel (orange or yellow).

**What to avoid:** stills held for more than 2 s (our first ep01 draft), a slow empty opening, changing voices between episodes (the top comment on Underwater Championship complains about it), and claims we can't back up.

## 4. What this changes for Sorty episode 1

- **Keep:** the hook on frame one ("Got 500 saved reels?"), Sorty's mask and cape, the outro.
- **Add a story, not a montage:** stakes (Sorty's boss, The Algorithm, says "4,000 saves sorted by Friday or you're fired") → the question (can he?) → the twist (he doesn't sort them himself; he drops the zip into SaveSort, and the real screen shows 156 saves sorted) → the rehook ("Episode 2 Friday: Sorty vs your recipe saves").
- **Light it like the winners:** dark blue phone-at-night for the problem, warm gold for the win.
- **CTA:** "Comment SORT and I'll DM you the link." Use it only once the auto-DM is live; until then, use "savesortai.com, link in bio".
- **Make it as a series:** every episode has a title card ("Ep 1: The 4,000 Saves") and the same voice.
