## Experiment ideas (general public, marketing-focused, no fake people or testimonials)

- `exp-pov`: relatable POV ("POV: you open your saved tab looking for one recipe").
- `exp-before-after`: the messy saved tab, then the sorted SaveSort list.
- `exp-myth-fact`: one viral claim from a saved reel, then what's actually true.
- `exp-challenge`: "I did one saved reel a day for a week" style, only with real, checkable results.
- `exp-signs`: "3 signs your saved tab is a junk drawer".
- `exp-question`: asks viewers one question to comment ("How many saves do you have?").
- `exp-stitch`: reacts to a common type of reel (paraphrased, no real creator named).

## Style results so far (7 Nov 2026)

- `base`: 16 judged reels, median 116 total views, 3.8% engagement (engagement is mostly the same 2 likes on every reel, so ignore it). Instagram is the useful signal: saves-led reels opening on a concrete problem with Sorty talking got 70–121 IG views and ~6 s watched (`watch-it-sort`, `junk-drawer-fix`, `guess-30`, `find-that-reel`); tip-led reels 5–19 IG views. This week's white-title cold opens got 27–46 IG views (see `content/lessons.md`).
- `funny-ad`: 1 judged (`rip-saved-tab`, 142 views, IG 44 / 3.2 s); `pov-later` (2 days old) IG 27 / 3.3 s. Not the worst style yet. Keep 4 slots.
- Hook types (IG avg watch): stakes 6.3–6.5 s (2 reels), question 4.5–4.7 s (2), crazy-* 1.4–6.2 s (4, median 3.7 s), bold-claim 3.6 s (1). Use more stakes-style lines.
- TikTok gives every reel ~70–135 views and 1–2.5 s watched whatever it is, with 0 follows so far; don't use TikTok to compare styles.
