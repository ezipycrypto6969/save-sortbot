## Experiment ideas (general public, marketing-focused, no fake people or testimonials)

- `exp-pov`: relatable POV ("POV: you open your saved tab looking for one recipe").
- `exp-before-after`: the messy saved tab, then the sorted SaveSort list.
- `exp-myth-fact`: one viral claim from a saved reel, then what's actually true.
- `exp-challenge`: "I did one saved reel a day for a week" style, only with real, checkable results.
- `exp-signs`: "3 signs your saved tab is a junk drawer".
- `exp-question`: asks viewers one question to comment ("How many saves do you have?").
- `exp-stitch`: reacts to a common type of reel (paraphrased, no real creator named).

## Style results so far (14 Nov 2026)

- `base`: 25 judged, median 137 total views. Best: the guess reels (`guess-useful` 129 IG / 5.3 s, `guess-30` 116 / 5.8 s) and how-to reels with a broad search title (60–82 YouTube views: `export-saves`, `junk-drawer`, `opened-none`, `watched-vs-saved`). Narrow "find your recipe / gift" reels were weak.
- `funny-ad`: 4 judged, median 112 total views, the lower regular style this week (first week as worst; cut only if worst again next week). Fine on Instagram at 07:00 (`mate-40` 69, `lost-and-found` 59) but 1–3 YouTube views: their titles weren't searches. Next week: 07:00 and a how-to `yt_title`.
- Hook types (IG avg watch, judged): guess 5.3–5.8 s (2, plus 4.9 s early), stakes 3.3–6.5 s, question 3.3–4.6 s, confession 3.1–3.2 s (but `10-lied` 5.2 s early), pov 2.8 s (1), crazy-* 1.4–3.8 s on IG. On TikTok the crazy warning held best (5.0 s, 1 reel).
- TikTok gives most reels ~50–200 views and 1–2.6 s watched whatever they are; 1 follower ever. Don't use TikTok views to compare styles.
