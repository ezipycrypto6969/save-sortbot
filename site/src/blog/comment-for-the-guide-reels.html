You've seen the format. A reel shows an impressive result, then the caption says: comment "GUIDE" and I'll send you the full steps. You save it for later. Later, the reel still doesn't contain the steps.

We call these gated guides. In our founder's 156 saved reels, 22 were gated: about one in seven.

Why creators do it

  • Comments help a reel spread. Comments are one of the signals Instagram uses when deciding how widely to show a post. A reel that asks everyone to comment collects a lot of them.
  • It opens a private message with you. The reply is normally sent automatically by a comment-to-DM tool. Once you're in the creator's messages, they can follow up later.
  • The guide is often a lead magnet. Sometimes it's a useful free document. Sometimes it's a page that asks for your email, or a pitch for a paid course.

None of that makes a gated reel bad. Plenty of them point to real, useful tools. It just means the reel you saved is an advert for the guide, not the guide itself.

What happens when you comment

Usually you get an automated message within a minute or so, with a link. Some creators ask you to follow them first. If nothing arrives, the automation may have been switched off, which is common with older reels.

That's the real problem with saving these: by the time you come back to your Saved tab, the comment trick may not work any more.

How to find the method without commenting

  1. Read the whole caption. The name of the tool is often right there. One of our founder's saves said to comment a word for "the GitHub repo", and named the project in the next sentence.
  2. Search the name. Search for the tool or project by name, and add "GitHub" if it's code. In that example the project was public and free, so no comment was needed.
  3. Check the creator's link in bio. Free guides are often linked there as well.
  4. Watch for the catch. If the caption promises income ("$1,000 a day") and hides every detail, treat it as hype until proven otherwise.
