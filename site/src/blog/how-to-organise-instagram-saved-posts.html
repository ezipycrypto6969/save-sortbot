Saving a reel takes one tap. Finding it again three months later takes ten minutes of scrolling, if you bother at all. The Saved tab behind our sample report held 156 reels, in the order they were saved and nothing else.

Here are three ways to get a Saved tab under control, from the least effort to the most thorough.

1. Use Instagram's own collections

Instagram lets you group saves into private folders called collections. Only you can see them.

  1. Go to your profile, open the menu and tap Saved.
  2. Tap the + to make a collection, give it a name and pick the saves that belong in it.
  3. From then on, press and hold the bookmark icon on any post to choose a collection as you save it.

Good for: new saves from today onwards. If you choose a collection every time you save, the mess never builds up.

The limit: it's manual. Instagram doesn't sort your existing saves for you, so a backlog of a few hundred reels means opening and filing them one by one. It also can't tell you which saves were worth keeping.

2. Do a ten-minute weekly clear-out

Most saves are only useful for a short while: a recipe for this weekend, a tool you wanted to try. A simple rule keeps the pile small.

  • Once a week, open Saved and look only at the newest row or two.
  • For each one, decide: do it this week, file it in a collection, or unsave it.
  • If you can't remember why you saved it, unsave it.

Good for: staying on top of things once you're organised. The limit: it doesn't help with the backlog you already have.

3. Export everything and sort it in one go

Instagram will give you a copy of your saves as a file. That file includes the link and caption of every post you've saved, which means the whole backlog can be sorted at once instead of one reel at a time.

  1. Ask Instagram for your saved posts (tick only Saved, format HTML). Instagram emails you a zip.
  2. Drop the zip into SaveSort. It reads only the saved-posts file inside, in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
  3. You get every save grouped by niche and by type (how-tos, tools, news, memes), with a label on each: Do it, Watch it, Skip it or Locked.

On those 156 saves, the sort found 30 worth doing, 65 where the caption said almost nothing, 10 that looked like hype and 22 gated guides. That turns "sort out my saves" into a list of 30 things to choose from.

What SaveSort doesn't do: it doesn't move anything around inside Instagram or create collections for you. It gives you a sorted list with a link back to each reel. The sort reads captions, so it's quick but not perfect.
